### 1. Reviewer-level assessment

**The job.** §4 has to show how a defense's leakage predicate becomes a finite suite of runnable tests in which every test carries a transmission the defense forbids. It must also back the two claims the AMuLeT comparison rests on: complete up to a bound, and targeted. The mechanics are there, but the completeness claim is overstated and reported without a single count, and the targeting argument is never stated. Stage 2's description leaves out the instruction vocabulary that three of the reported bugs depend on, and it describes two features the evaluation never uses.

**Weaknesses (as a PC reviewer would write them).**

- W1. "The paper claims Stage 1a runs 'to UNSAT for every defense except SPT' and outputs 'every backbone of up to six instructions', but it never reports how many backbones exist, how long enumeration took, or why six suffices." The project record shows that for STT and ReCon one six-instruction partition (branch transmitters) never finished (`URGENT_TODO.md:202`, DECIDE RL-6, open; `TWO_PHASE_MODELS.md:666`). SPT, per the Stage-1b decision LP-4, does not run this two-stage flow at all (`URGENT_TODO.md:252`).
- W2. "Completeness does not survive the pipeline. Stage 1b draws 'a capped number' of graphs per backbone in a non-uniform solver order, and Stage 2 samples again, so the claims that 'the suite contains every backbone up to the bound' and that 'every leakage backbone … gets at least one test' are not shown." The record says they are false: six footprint backbones yield no Stage-1b graph at all (`EVAL_MANIFEST.md:342`, "6 cores EXHAUSTED at 0").
- W3. "§4 never says which x86 instructions Stage 2 can emit, yet Results credit A-UV3 to read-modify-writes (results.tex:33), UV5 to 'once our synthesizer emits direct jumps' (results.tex:65), and UV1 to routing through floating-point registers (results.tex:46). Meanwhile §4 describes chain injection (Pass 4) and store bypass, which no evaluated corpus uses (`"interleave": {"enabled": false}`, `"data_flow": false` in both evaluated Stage-2 configs)."
- W4. "Stage boundaries are blurred. §4 says Stage 2 samples 'branch directions and resolution timing' and writes 'when each branch and store resolves', while §4.3.2 emits both directions and §5 says 'Timing directives come from the run configuration' (pipeline.tex:118). The Stage-2 timing paragraph repeats §5 and gives a 'fixed 5000-cycle stall' that Table 2 contradicts (2500, 5000 or 10000). Table 2 also labels the executor 'Stage 3', though §4 defines three stages."
- W5. "Minimality is sold as keeping the set small, but SPT's minimal graphs exceed 200,000 at four instructions, and Stage 1b then deliberately produces non-minimal graphs. What minimality buys, beyond defining coverage, is not argued. Moreover, CheckMate, which the paper credits for 'security litmus tests', defines them as minimal programs (CheckMate §III-B2, p.5); SimSpect's tests are not minimal, and the paper does not say so."
- W6. "No example follows a backbone through Stage 1b and Stage 2, so the reader never sees what a test looks like before Results." Compare rtl2µspec §4, which refers to "Fig. 3 throughout this section—a précis of the main stages of the synthesis procedure" (p.5), and Protean §V-A: "ProtCC features four passes (one trivial), whose outputs are shown in Fig. 3b-e for an example program in Fig. 3a" (p.6).

**Questions for the rebuttal.**

- Q1. How many backbones does each specification have, at what sizes, and how long does Stage 1a take? If every backbone found has at most three instructions, why is the bound six?
- Q2. Did every six-instruction partition reach UNSAT for STT and ReCon?
- Q3. Which generator produced the SPT tests behind the UV6 numbers: SPT's capped Stage 1a, SPT's single-stage model, an earlier SPT corpus (results.tex:69), or the STT corpus (`EVAL_MANIFEST.md:1355`)?
- Q4. How many extended graphs per backbone, and how is the cap set? Is the random solver order biased toward some shapes? Do some backbones get no test?
- Q5. Why must every Stage-1b graph contain a committed instruction, and why is the Stage-1b bound eight?
- Q6. What is the x86 vocabulary? Does compiling at `-O2` ever change branch layout (discussion.tex:29 says LLVM's block layout decides the speculative direction)?
- Q7. Does any reported result use store bypass?
- Q8. How do you know Stage 2's added instructions and xmm routing keep the graph's violation, i.e., that the compiled test still has the backbone's forbidden transmission?
