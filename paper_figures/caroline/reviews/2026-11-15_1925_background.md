### 2. Reviewer-level assessment

**The job.** §2 must give an architecture reader the minimum Spectre and defense mechanics to follow six defenses' bugs, and show where each of SimSpect's three clauses and its synthesis method come from, so the reader sees exactly what is borrowed and what is new. It does the first with about 40% padding. It muddles the second: seven overlapping contract terms, one of them misattributed. It undersells the third: CheckMate gets one clause, and that clause is inaccurate.

**Weaknesses (as a PC member would write them).**

- W1 (clarity, formalism). "In two paragraphs the background introduces observer modes, execution modes, observation clauses, execution clauses, AMuLeT's leakage contracts, SynthLC's leakage contracts and execution contracts. It then says SimSpect 'borrows two of these names in narrower senses', although neither borrowed name appears in the preceding paragraph. Fig. 1 then gives STT's leakage contract as 'CT with conditions', which is an observer mode. I could not tell whether a leakage contract is an observer mode or not."
- W2 (soundness of claims). "The paper grounds its leakage contract in SynthLC's 'fine-grained' contracts, but SynthLC reserves 'fine-grained' for the bespoke contracts STT adopts instead of a CT contract. What SimSpect writes for STT, (load, address) and (branch, condition), is SynthLC's canonical CT contract. Other source statements are off too: 'visibility point' is InvisiSpec's term, not STT's; SPT also declassifies operands that will leak, not only operands already leaked; and one InvisiSpec citation, to the wrong section, carries a scope claim about three defenses."
- W3 (novelty, positioning). "CheckMate already synthesizes Spectre security litmus tests with Alloy from axiomatic graphs. The only contrast offered, 'what it adds, speculation flags and a defense's leakage predicate', is inaccurate (SimSpect uses spo, not po, and CheckMate already models speculation) and is not an argument. The real difference is never stated: SimSpect models a defense's guarantee instead of a microarchitecture, and it tests an implementation instead of a model."
- W4 (focus). "About a third of the section is prior work the narrative never uses: Revizor, COND, speculative non-interference, NDA and SpecShield, and seven MCM formalizations (x86-TSO, ARMv8, C++11, Arm ifetch/VM/exceptions). Meanwhile, AMuLeT's method and its contract pairings are repeated from the introduction and Results."
- W5 (support). "The introduction cites §2.2 for 'No instruction-level contract can capture such a guarantee exactly', but §2.2 contains no such argument; the authors' own note at background.tex:23 says so. Under Guarnieri et al.'s framework, an adversary model restricted to one channel is the obvious counterexample."
- W6 (what is missing, consistency). "Definitions conflict across sections. 'Transient' means bound to squash here but 'uncommitted' in the abstract and introduction. Transmitter, speculation primitive, minimality and relaxation are each defined two or three times. Fig. 1's STT example uses 'SpectreCtrl' and 'unaccessed memory', which no section defines. The paper has no threat model."

**Questions a reviewer would ask in the rebuttal.**
