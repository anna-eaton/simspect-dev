**Weaknesses (as a PC reviewer would write them).**

- W1. "§5.2 defines the violation window as lasting while 'every branch the graph marks unresolved is still unresolved, i.e., before its squash lands', then says the Inspector checks it only 'up to each such branch's resolution' and that a separate scan covers 'the cycles between the resolution and the squash'. - W2. "The window rule is false for InvisiSpec, the defense with the most reported detections: the paper runs it in its Futuristic model, where the Inspector counts a load's change before its visibility point whatever the window (`check_footprint.py` `before_visibility_point`; `invisispec.json` maps `FuturisticSafeInvisibleSpec` to it). The paper never says so."
- W3. "The invisible-speculation criteria 'count changes to the cache structures their papers protect'. CleanupSpec's paper lets speculative loads change the cache and undoes the changes (Background), so as written every CleanupSpec test is a hit; the D-TLB, which Fig. 5's InvisiSpec criterion counts, is not a cache structure, and Background says all three defenses protect 'only the data-cache hierarchy'."
- W4. "Several harness choices have no reason the reader can check: why three hold lengths, why the invisible-speculation runs draw a random cache state, why ReCon starts warm ('because they are not checking cache transmitters', although its transmitters are loads that touch the cache)." AMuLeT gives its own reset rule with a reason (§3.5); the defense papers give one too (CleanupSpec §1 handles installs and evictions differently; SpecLFB §9 leaves hits out of scope).

"The result vocabulary is still loose: a 'hit' is 'a test whose run meets the defense's transmission criterion', which drops the window and the validity check that §5.2 adds, and a 'leak' is a hit 'we confirm, on its trace', with no rule for confirming.
