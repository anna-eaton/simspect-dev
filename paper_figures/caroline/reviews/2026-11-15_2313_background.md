**The job.** §2 must give an architecture reader just enough Spectre-defense mechanics to follow six defenses' bugs, and show where each of SimSpect's three clauses and its synthesis method come from, so the reader can separate what is borrowed from what is new. **Weaknesses (as a PC member would write them).**

- W1 (clarity). "The background never defines 'speculative', yet uses it in at least three senses (before STT's visibility point, CleanupSpec's wrong-path 'transient', and Spectector's 'speculative executions'). 'Safe' means untainted in one sentence and 'at the visibility point' in the next. 'Minimal' means fewest micro-ops for CheckMate and relaxation-minimal for Lustig, two sentences apart. One speculation model has four names across the paper ('Spectre model', 'until all older branches resolve', 'SpectreCtrl', 'the Spectre configuration')."
- W2 (soundness). "The background asserts that a contract's adversary model 'cannot differentiate between different transmitters that touch the same microarchitectural state', with no citation or argument, one paragraph after calling the adversary model separate from the contract; the introduction leans on this subsection for a universal claim ('No instruction-level contract can capture such a guarantee exactly'). Figure 1 glosses ARCH-SEQ as 'also known as weak speculative non-interference', but the background itself defines wSNI as a program property relative to ARCH-SEQ."
- W3 (novelty, positioning). "CheckMate synthesized Spectre security litmus tests with Alloy from axiomatic graphs. The only contrast offered, that SimSpect 'adds speculation flags', is wrong (CheckMate's case study is a speculative out-of-order processor), and the real differences (a defense's guarantee instead of a processor model; forbidden leaks instead of allowed exploits; tests run on an implementation) are never stated."
- W4 (focus). "About 30% of the section is prior work nothing later uses: COND and Revizor, a two-sentence SNI/wSNI definition, GLIFT, seven MCM formalizations, and an informal justification of acyclicity. Meanwhile AMuLeT's method and contract pairings are repeated from the introduction and Results."
- W5 (what is missing). "There is no threat model, no statement of which speculation model each tested defense build uses (AMuLeT ran InvisiSpec and STT in Futuristic mode, so the comparison depends on it), and no statement of which instructions transmit which operands when, though the STT specification and the Inspector rely on all three."

"The specification's clause named 'execution contract' is never related to anything in the background: its name echoes Guarnieri et al.'s execution mode, but the introduction defines it as Protean's speculation model.
