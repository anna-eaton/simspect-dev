### 1. Reviewer-level assessment

**The job.** §3 must define the paper's first contribution, the specification language, precisely enough that a reader can check each of the six defenses' specifications against its paper and see exactly what the synthesizer enumerates. It now does this for STT and states the predicate cleanly; it falls short on the other specifications, where the text does not match the models that were run (ReCon's execution contract, SPT's registers), the specification count contradicts itself, and the transmission criterion is attached to two different objects.

**Weaknesses (as a PC reviewer would write them).**

- W1. "§3.3 says ReCon's execution contract makes an instruction speculative after an unresolved branch or an unresolved store, but the artifact's ReCon models each use one primitive: `Recon_6_u_lpP` branches only, `Recon_6_u_stl_lpP` stores only. Which specification produced the ReCon results?"
- W2. "The transmission criterion is said to belong to each leakage contract (§3.2), yet InvisiSpec, CleanupSpec and SpecLFB 'share one specification' and 'differ only in their transmission criteria' (§3.3). The §3 opening then calls it the predicate's criterion, although §3.2 says it is not part of the predicate. Where does the criterion live?"
- W3. "'The other five specifications' (§3.3) implies six specifications, but three of the six defenses share one, so there are four. The paper's own count is inconsistent."
- W4. "SPT's specification protects only registers the graph writes, although SPT 'taints all program data (memory and architectural registers)' (SPT §6.3), while the invisible-speculation specification protects exactly the registers the graph never writes. Neither choice is justified, and calling SPT's specification 'conservative' covers only its untaint rule; its register set errs the other way."
- W5. "The model section talks about tests, traces, the synthesizer and the Inspector (§3 opening, §3.1, §3.2, §3.3) before any of them exists."
- W6. "'Speculative program order' is the central order of the graph but is never defined; LCM's analogous transient fetch order is defined in one clause (LCM §3.3)."

**Questions for the rebuttal.**

- Q1. Did any evaluated ReCon model use the union of branch and store speculation? If not, why are the two primitives enumerated separately, and is that equivalent?
- Q2. Is the transmission criterion per defense or per leakage contract?
- Q3. Why does SPT's protection set exclude registers with no producer in the graph, when SPT §6.3 taints all registers initially?
- Q4. Is "the synthesizer's only defense-specific input" true for SPT, whose Stage 2 drops the ALU forms SPT untaints backward (`stage2_final_A_spt_*.jsonc`)?
- Q5. What is spo for a store-bypass graph: fetch order, or program order plus flags?

**Restructuring.** §3.3 does two jobs: the STT example (its heading) and a summary of the other three specifications (which Results and Discussion cite as "§3.3"). Split the paragraph at "The other five specifications" and retitle the subsection so it names both (item 11). No other reordering: the roadmap, the vocabulary, the clauses, the box and the example are in the right order, and each paragraph has one goal.
