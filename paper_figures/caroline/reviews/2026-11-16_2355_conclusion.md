### 1. Reviewer-level assessment

**The job.** The Conclusion has to leave an ISCA reader with the paper's claim in one breath: what SimSpect is (a specification language, a synthesizer and an executor that test a simulated Spectre defense against its own security guarantee), what it found, and what that shows. Line 4 states what SimSpect is and what it found. It does not say what the results show. It spends a fifth of its words on an Inspector rule and a fourth on three limits that §7 already states.

**Weaknesses (as a hostile PC member would write them).**

- W1. "The conclusion reports 3.7×–1,655× fewer tests per detection. But the low end rests on 5 detections against AMuLeT's 4, with a 95% interval of 0.8–19× (Fig. 11 caption), so for A-UV5 it is not fewer. And it leaves out that AMuLeT detects two of the five bugs faster in wall-clock time (§6.4, results.tex:85)." Evidence: results.tex:82, :85; Fig. 11 caption (`tikz/firstdet_bars_v9_8pt.tex:11`).

- W3. "The executor's key idea, steering each test toward its target execution through white-box directives, is missing. - W4. "The paper ends on three caveats, one of them in a term the paper never defines ('the instruction vocabulary'), all repeated from §7." Evidence: discussion.tex:33, :45, :48; model.tex:36 says "execution graph vocabulary"; no "instruction vocabulary" anywhere else.
- W5. "Small precision slips in the last paragraph: 'six simulated Spectre defenses' vs the intro's seven gem5 builds (intro:306), and 'first detect' vs the tests-per-detection estimate that Fig. 11 plots."

**Questions a reviewer would ask in the rebuttal.**

- Q1. Is A-UV5's 3.7× different from parity? (Fig. 11 caption: interval 0.8–19×.)
- Q2. Is UV6 a bug under gem5's default configuration? (No: it needs `commitToIEWDelay` ≥ 3, results.tex:75, discussion.tex:25.)
- Q3. How much of the reduction comes from the targeted tests, and how much from forcing mispredictions? (results.tex:82: "Two factors contribute, and we do not separate them".)

**Restructuring.** Keep four sentences, in this order: (1) what SimSpect tests; (2) how, naming all three parts as the abstract does, with the executor's steering; (3) the results, known bugs then new ones, as in the intro and Results; (4) one sentence that is either an insight, a takeaway, or future work.
