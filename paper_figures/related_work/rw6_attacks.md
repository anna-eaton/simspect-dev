## Proposed LaTeX paragraph (~140 words)

```latex
Since Spectre~\cite{spectre} and Meltdown~\cite{lipp2018meltdown}, transient-execution attacks have multiplied: data-sampling attacks that leak from in-flight buffers~\cite{vanbulck2018foreshadow,vanschaik2019ridl,schwarz2019zombieload,canella2019fallout,moghimi2023downfall}, injection attacks~\cite{vanbulck2020lvi}, and attacks that mistrain return and indirect-branch predictors~\cite{maisuradze2018ret2spec,koruyeh2018spectrersb,barberis2022bhi,wikner2022retbleed,trujillo2023inception,wikner2023phantom,li2024indirector}, often bypassing deployed mitigations; surveys systematize them~\cite{canella2019systematic,xiong2021survey,fiolhais2023transient}. Speculation is not limited to control flow. Spectre~v4~\cite{horn2018ssb} exploits memory-dependence prediction~\cite{moshovos1997dynamic,chrysos1998storesets}, and recent work keeps finding new leaks in store-to-load forwarding and load prediction: AMD's predictive store forwarding~\cite{amd2021psf,liu2024amdpredictors}, Intel and Arm dependence predictors~\cite{liu2026pitfall,liu2026ssbleed,liu2025mdpeek}, and Apple's load address and value predictors~\cite{kim2025slap,kim2025flop}. SSBench~\cite{liu2026ssbench} characterizes these predictors automatically. SimSpect's STL shapes target this class, testing whether defenses actually contain store-bypass speculation.
```
