## Proposed LaTeX paragraph (~180 words)

```latex
\paragraph{Execution models of speculation.}
A line of work gives speculation a formal semantics. At the program level, operational and axiomatic models capture speculative evaluation~\cite{disselkoen2019code,cheang2019formal,colvin2020abstractsemantics}, often with an always-mispredict semantics~\cite{guarnieri2020spectector}, and support verified software defenses~\cite{cauligi2022sok}. Cats vs.\ Spectre~\cite{ponce2022cats} extends axiomatic memory models with speculative relations to find attacks on a baseline processor. Closer to hardware, InSpectre~\cite{guanciale2020inspectre} gives an operational out-of-order semantics, abstract processor models are model-checked for speculation properties~\cite{cabodi2019speculationmc}, and Pensieve~\cite{pensieve} checks early-stage defense designs against speculative non-interference. RTL2M$\mu$PATH~\cite{rtl2upath} synthesizes $\mu$PATH models bottom-up from RTL. Defenses such as STT come with pen-and-paper proofs over an abstract machine~\cite{yu2019sttformal}. \toolname{} differs in two ways. Its Alloy model is not a full execution semantics but an abstract snapshot of microarchitectural state (resolved and committed flags, the protection set, and transmitters) that encodes the defense's own guarantee. And instead of proving that model secure, \toolname{} uses it to synthesize tests that check whether the implementation actually realizes it.
```
