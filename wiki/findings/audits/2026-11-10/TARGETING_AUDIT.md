# Targeting audit: is any part of the pipeline steered toward a known bug? (2026-11-10)

Read-only audit (agent targeting-audit, for Anna). Question: beyond generic, better fanout, does anything in the generator, harness, checkers or classifiers target a specific known bug (AMuLeT's UV1, UV3, UV4, UV5, UV6) or one of the bugs the paper claims as new (STT xmm4, ReCon STL forwarding, ReCon OTB, SpecLFB jmp shadow, walk order, the visibility-point/squash race)? Project rule: "DO NOT go tuning the pipeline to hit or avoid certain bug cases ... only tuning to fix wrong things". Nothing was edited, run or committed; every claim cites the file it comes from.

## Answer first

- **Targeted (fix, drop, or keep out of the reported numbers):** (1) the fanout_v2 **OTB merge**, which builds the getOldestTaint gadget by construction and is in today's first-detection corpus; (2) the **jmp post-pass** `make_jmp_corpus.py`, which puts a direct jmp exactly where jmp shadow needs one (not yet in any reported number); (3) the **pinned jmp/RMW corpus** (`jmprmw_pin.py`, `STT_6_rmwjmp`); (4) the S4/A3c and OTB **forcing models** (`STT_6_s4`, `STT_6_a3c`, `STT_6_multisource`, the `otb` queries, bugsynth's forced OTB). Items 3-4 feed no eval corpus today and must stay that way. (5) Borderline: the loose-pin minimality exemption `lp_relevant_br`, written from the S4 and A3c shapes and now in the decided final corpus (LP-1).
- **Defensible, but the paper must disclose:** FP (xmm) routing on every consumed load, added after a source audit found xmm4; the RMW transmitter rewrite, added for UV3; `jmp_direct`, added after jmp shadow was found; the `inc`/`cmov_rr` forms copied from the SPT A1/A2 audit shapes; the `cold` and `full` cache setups, built after UV3 and UV1 were not found warm, and reporting each bug under its best setup; the slow-commit hook behind the race; SimSpect-only window widening (BTB force, 5000-cycle resolve stall); signatures that differ between the SimSpect and AMuLeT classifiers; and a CPU-time comparison that leaves out SimSpect's generation cost. Also: the loose-pin design was judged by how many UV5/OTB shapes it produces.
- **General (no issue):** the defense blocks of every model, including the three new footprint models; splices and fillers; random register naming; the seeded solver order; the checkers and the leakage JSONs (paper-quoted); the hooks (observation only, byte-identical when off); TMPDIR; and the H-PAD, SETUP and own-RMW exclusions, which only lower SimSpect's counts.
- **Discovery order (needed for every "new bug" claim):** jmp shadow first showed up in the plain STT_6 corpus with no jmp-specific stimulus (claudelog 2026-11-08 hooks-v2, 144+4 taken-mode hits). The walk order showed up in the same runs. xmm4 (S1) was found by **code reading** (claudelog 2026-11-08 17:30 src-audit) and only then confirmed by a pipeline that was extended for it (19:20 fpr-fanout), but `BUGS.md:55` says "simspect tests". OTB was first produced only by forced shapes (bugsynth `otb_class`, claudelog 2026-06-09 "otb_class forcing is fundamentally necessary") and today by the OTB merge. The race shows up only under a non-stock slow-commit hook. ReCon STL forwarding (R-SA-9) is credited to Anna (`BUGS.md:60`).

## What the paper should disclose

1. **Bug-motivated vocabulary.** FP (xmm) routing was added after a source audit found xmm4; the RMW transmitter rewrite was added to reach UV3; `jmp_direct` was added after jmp shadow appeared in the plain corpus. Each is uniform, value-neutral vocabulary over all tests, but the order of events must be stated, and UV3 detection depends on the RMW rewrite.
2. **ReCon OTB comes from a mechanism-shaped transform** (the fanout_v2 OTB merge, and before it bugsynth's forced shape), unless the merge is generalized first (S2). Do not present OTB as an unsteered discovery.
3. **Where each claimed new bug came from:** jmp shadow and walk order from the unsteered corpus; xmm4 from code reading, then the pipeline; ReCon STL forwarding from Anna; OTB from forcing; the race only under the slow-commit stall.
4. **Harness differences from AMuLeT:** BTB force + resolve stall on every SimSpect test; four cache setups, one declared headline setup per defense with the rest as sensitivity; InvisiSpec UV1 under `full` fires on every test, so "1 test" measures the setup.
5. **Comparison mechanics:** per-bug signatures differ between the two classifiers; the estimators differ; SimSpect CPU time leaves out generation unless amortized.
6. **Model design:** the loose-pin minimality keeps extra branches by a relevance rule (state it in general terms, or replace it); the shape census that justified the loose pin counted UV5/OTB preconditions.
7. **Pinned or forced corpora** (jmp/RMW pin, S4/A3c, OTB forcing), if mentioned at all, are validation probes, not part of the detection numbers.
